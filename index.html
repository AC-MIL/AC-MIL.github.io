AC-MIL

Anatomy-Aware Dual-Stream Multiple Instance Learning
for Kidney Function Prediction

Abteen ArabMaziar Riazy*Ali Bashashati*

University of British Columbia · Vancouver, Canada

*Co-corresponding authors

Paper

AC-MIL is an anatomy-aware dual-stream multiple instance learning framework for predicting estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) from kidney whole-slide images. It represents each slide as two bags of patches: glomerular-enriched and non-glomerular. This separation preserves compartment-specific information that may be diluted when all tissue is pooled into a single representation.

A shared frozen CONCHv1.5 encoder embeds both bags. Each stream is pooled independently, and a bidirectional gating module integrates the resulting representations. Both pooled embeddings condition each gate, allowing the model to modulate features from either compartment using the context of the whole slide.

01

Anatomical partitioning

Extract 512 × 512 patches at 20× magnification. Frozen segmentation models separate them into glomerular-enriched and non-glomerular bags.

02

Compartment-specific pooling

A shared frozen encoder embeds both bags. Mean pooling, ABMIL, or TransMIL produces one pooled representation per compartment.

03

Bidirectional gating

Both pooled representations condition two feature-wise gates. The gated embeddings are concatenated and passed to a regression head to predict eGFR.

Disease-specific experiments

Performance by disease group

The best-performing single-stream compartment differs between disease groups: glomerular-enriched tissue in diabetic kidney disease, and non-glomerular tissue in acute tubular injury and acute interstitial nephritis. AC-MIL achieves the highest Pearson correlation in both groups.

Pathology analysis

Exploratory analysis of tissue morphology

High-attention patches from each stream are examined using a vision-language model, comparing patients with high and low predicted eGFR.

Glomerular morphologic features

The low-predicted-eGFR group has higher frequencies of VLM-identified inflammation, fibrosis or matrix expansion, injury, and structural distortion. VLM-identified inflammation is reported in 14.0% of patches in this group, compared with 4.2% in the high-predicted-eGFR group.

Non-glomerular morphologic features

VLM-identified injury or degeneration is more frequent in the high-predicted-eGFR group than in the low-predicted-eGFR group (9.9% versus 2.3%). The observed association differs from that in glomerular patches.
